2. Methodology summary

Pressure = top 20% of deliveries (within each innings) by leverage, the expected win-probability swing on that ball. Rating = 0.6 × posterior pressure skill + 0.2 × observed pressure performance + 0.2 × consistency.

Data

867 matches, about 201,000 deliveries (401 IPL fixtures and 482 T20I fixtures in matches.csv). Innings 1 and 2 only. Wides, no-balls and "other" no-ball codes are illegal deliveries. After excluding no-results, ties and super-over decisions: 189,984 scored deliveries from 804 matches.

Defining pressure

  • Gradient-boosted win-probability model per innings (out-of-fold, 5-fold grouped by match, recalibrated). Chase Brier score 0.146 vs 0.249 baseline.
  • Leverage = expected absolute win-probability change over the outcomes dot, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, wicket.
  • High = top 20% within each innings (37,998 deliveries). Mean leverage is 2.83 pp per ball in High vs 0.34 in Low (about 8×).
  • When chasing under pressure: strike rate 143.8 vs 137.8, dismissals 6.7 vs 5.7 per 100 balls, dots 35.0% vs 38.7%.

Measuring performance

  • Run value per ball: runs minus wicket cost (resource-curve, about 6 runs on average, 9–15 early and 2–5 late). Bowlers get the mirror image.
  • Adjusted against the league average in the same innings × phase × leverage-decile cell (60 cells), plus a small opposition-strength (Elo) adjustment.
  • Baseline = the player’s own performance outside the High tier.

Rating and weights

Weights were chosen using out-of-sample prediction across 40 random half-splits. Pressure-only performance alone is barely repeatable for batters (split-half 0.05; bowlers 0.18) versus ordinary performance (0.43 and 0.23), so shrinkage is heavy. Final blend predicts held-out pressure performance with Spearman 0.20 (batters) and 0.24 (bowlers).

Fair comparison and limits

  • Eligibility: ≥100 pressure balls, ≥12 pressure innings, ≥100 ordinary balls. 102 batters and 72 bowlers qualify.
  • Robust to modelling choices (opposition adjustment, weights, eligibility) but less so to which matches are used (IPL only, T20I only, 2024 onward).
  • Limits: win-probability model ignores team quality and conditions; first-innings model is weak; wicket cost is approximate; bye and leg-bye runs assumed 1.
  • Abhishek Sharma (rank 2) is the most debatable placement: excellent ordinary-level output, only slightly above average under pressure.

5. IPL 2027 auction: impact players available (age-adjusted)

SupeRR Selector Season 2, Round 1 add-on. Pool = players released before the 16 Dec 2026 mini-auction who appear in our pressure data. Traded and retained players are excluded.

How the ranking works

Impact score = Pressure score (our main model: 0.6 posterior pressure skill + 0.2 observed pressure performance + 0.2 consistency, z-scored) + age adjustment: +0.50 for age 26 or under, +0.25 for 27 to 29, 0 for 30 to 32, −0.25 for 33 to 34, −0.50 for 35 and over. Younger players keep their value for several seasons and are more likely to attract bids; older players lose it. RV = runs above league average in the same match context per 100 balls under pressure. * = fewer than 100 pressure balls or 12 pressure innings (less reliable). Ages are approximate (Nov 2026) from public birth dates; the dataset has no ages.
